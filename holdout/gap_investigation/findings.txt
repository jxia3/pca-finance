Missing-return investigation, November 20, 2026

Scope

This review examines the retained source archive and official SEC, exchange,
and issuer records. No new market-price datasets were downloaded. Archived
scripts were read as text, not executed. The return files, strategy parameters,
report and saved strategy results remain unchanged.

There are no entirely missing date rows. The file has 723 market dates and
1,201 stock columns. The issue is 37,336 missing stock-date observations.
This is a targeted investigation of major gaps and portfolio-relevant cases,
not a claim that every missing cell has now been explained.

1. Many blanks follow real corporate events

The existing evidence identifies 55 cutoffs, accounting for 18,018 blanks.
This review identifies 11 additional termination cases: SRT, CPE, AMNB, MDC,
CVLY, HIBB, EMKR, SASR, JWN, FFIC and PDCO. Their post-event periods account
for another 5,607 missing cells. Together these categories account for 23,625
cells, or 63.3% of all blanks. The remaining 13,711 cells are outside these
reviewed cutoff periods, not necessarily missing trading observations.

For example, SRT stops on January 4, 2024 and ceased trading before the next
session because of its take-private transaction. AMNB and CPE both stop on
March 28, 2024, before their April 1 acquisitions. These endpoints make sense.
The old audit's unresolved category was broader than an actual provider-error
category. Confirming a merger does not reconstruct its cash/share consideration
or prove when an investor could have known the last trading date.

Individual dates, interpretations and official sources are in reviewed_events.csv.

2. A missed ticker change is a specific recovery opportunity

NICK's last observed return is September 27, 2024. The issuer announced that
its name changed to Old Market Capital and its symbol changed to OMCC at the
September 30 open. All 536 subsequent stock-date cells are blank under the
old identifier. This strongly indicates an unhandled symbol continuation,
rather than evidence that the equity disappeared. A verified OMCC series and
corporate-action bridge are needed before accepting replacement returns.

Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1000045/000095017024109986/nick-ex99_1.htm

3. Exchange delisting does not necessarily mean the stock stopped existing

Official filings confirm OTC continuation for several affected stocks:
- DYNT: Nasdaq to OTCQB on July 9, 2024. The local series stops July 12.
- SFES: Nasdaq/SFE to OTCQX/SFES on February 12, 2024. A 219-session gap
  begins that day, followed by resumed accepted observations.
- DXYN: Nasdaq suspension and OTC trading from October 3, 2024. Its
  97-session gap begins exactly at that transition.
- PTN: NYSE suspension followed by PTNT OTC trading from May 8, 2025,
  then announced relisting in November. The archive explicitly withheld this
  interval for ticker and reverse-split reconstruction. Returns resume later.
- SUP: OTC trading as SSUP from June 25, 2025, exactly when the gap begins.
  Subsequent merger/settlement events require further reconstruction.

SALM's issuer announcement also anticipated a January 2024 OTC transition.
These documents establish event context. They do not independently validate
every subsequent quote or explain every later missing cell.

4. Some prices exist in the archive but fail the acceptance rules

Across AGM.A, AKO.A, CYAN, DXYN, SFES and PTN, all 1,948 missing return cells
have current and preceding-session closes in the retained Yahoo response.
That does not make those closes suitable replacements. The earlier historical
comparison accepted only 16.4% of AGM.A return differences within five basis
points, versus the standard 95% threshold. The corresponding figures were
82.2% for AKO.A, 62.0% for CYAN, 60.8% for DXYN and 68.2% for PTN.

The fallback Dolt prices pass historical checks for these names, with a reviewed
short-overlap exception for AGM.A. But for AGM.A and AKO.A, none of the remaining
missing returns has both consecutive closes in the retained Dolt bulk extract.
Thus usable source coverage is sparse even though another source supplies
apparently complete prices. A missing close also prevents calculating the next
session's return. The pipeline intentionally does not bridge multi-session
price changes or invent zero returns.

For CYAN, DXYN and SFES, later OTC recovery requires two consecutive prices
corroborated by the retained secondary table. This table lacks the needed pair
of closes for 394 of CYAN's 395 gaps, all 210 DXYN gaps, and all 402 SFES gaps.
These are limitations of retained corroborating coverage, not evidence that
no trade occurred. No new requests were made to those secondary providers.

The remaining CYAN case is December 2, 2025: saved prices rise from 0.35 to
0.574, a 64% change. Both saved sources agree, but the OTC recovery routine
requires an absolute daily return no greater than 50%, so this candidate is
excluded. This is a specific review-filter effect. The observation should be
reviewed for identity and corporate actions, not automatically accepted.

5. Temporary gaps are concentrated

Of 924 missing spells, 809 resume, covering 3,146 missing cells. AGM.A, SFES,
CYAN, AKO.A and DXYN account for 1,795 of these cells, or 57.1%. This helps
explain why most spells are temporary while most missing cells overall occur
in long trailing gaps. Counting spells and counting cells answer different
questions. Neither supports treating every blank as permanent delisting.

6. A separate accepted-data problem needs correction review

Nasdaq gives April 16, 2025 as PDCO's last trading day and says it was halted
on April 17. The accepted file nevertheless contains zero returns on April 17
and April 21, attributed to the Defeatbeta bulk archive. These cannot be treated
as evidence of executable exchange prices. post_event_observations.csv records
both entries. The existing TB simulation holds and trades PDCO on these dates.
The records alone do not establish why the vendor retained those values.

Source: https://classic.nasdaqtrader.com/TraderNews.aspx?id=ECA2025-195

IVAC also has a zero return on its March 31 merger date. That case remains
flagged for exact last-trading-time review rather than assumed erroneous.

7. What the interrupted download does and does not establish

The saved transfer log confirms a connection closed before a bulk ZIP was
complete. The collection retained complete recovered rows and used additional
sources to recover observations. This is a real acquisition limitation, but
the log does not attribute every current blank to that interruption. In
particular, the diagnosed mergers, ticker changes, OTC gaps and validation
exclusions cannot be reduced to a single interrupted-download explanation.

Local evidence: raw/recovery/dolt_download.json in source_evidence.zip.

Implications for the saved holdout

Both final frozen pairs holdings now have specific event leads. FFIC, about
0.985% of final equity, merged into OceanFirst with consideration of 0.85 OCFC
shares per FFIC share. SUP, about 0.975%, initially continued as SSUP on OTC.
Freezing both indefinitely therefore omits different economic paths. Neither
case justifies assuming an observed zero return or a 100% loss.

The existing results remain conditional marks, not final realized P&L. The
highest-priority repairs are to resolve these two held securities, correct
the PDCO executable-date boundary, investigate NICK/OMCC continuity, and expand
the event table. Further quote recovery should use verified security mappings
and trustworthy data, with corporate actions checked before accepting returns.
Any repaired-data evaluation should preserve fixed parameters and be recorded
as a data correction, rather than silently replacing the original run.

Source: https://www.nasdaqtrader.com/TraderNews.aspx?id=ECA2026-362

Reproduction

Run from the repository root:
  python -m scripts.audit_holdout_gaps

Outputs are summary.json, gap_spells.csv, per_stock.csv,
cached_quote_diagnostics.csv and post_event_observations.csv. The manually
reviewed official-source annotations are in reviewed_events.csv. The script
makes no network requests and does not change source data or run strategies.
